# Hatching and chalk style decision brief

Context: Anteon film, chalk white #eceae4 strokes on board #0b0b0d, wide landscape, converting multi-directional scribble to parallel directional hatching. Our marks are light and bare board is shadow, so classical ink logic inverts.

## 1. Default hatch angle

Use one fixed diagonal at about 30° from horizontal, descending left to right (upper-left to lower-right). The drafting and engraving convention centers on 45° with either diagonal treated as valid, so any clean single diagonal reads as deliberate shading rather than as loose lines (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatching). Handedness sets the natural stroke. Right-handed hatching runs ascending left to right, and Leonardo's authenticated left-handed hatching is the descending mirror, a trait consistent enough to help date his sheets (getty.edu/news/leonardo-da-vinci-renaissance-lefty). For a wide landscape frame, shade shallower than 45° (about 30°, band 25 to 40°) so the parallels rhyme with the horizon and stay calm instead of busy. Default to the descending direction, which flows with left-to-right, top-to-bottom reading and reads as light raking from the conventional upper-left source, suiting marks that are themselves the light. The ascending right-handed mirror is an equal alternative. The one non-negotiable is a single fixed angle held across the film.

## 2. Deviation policy

Hold the fixed angle for board, sky, ground, cast shadow, and flat base tone; let the hatch bend to follow the form (contour hatching) only on primary rounded volumes where roundness must read. This is the hybrid Dürer uses, with flat horizontal hatch in skies, curved hatch wrapping drapery, and dense crosshatch reserved for the deepest pockets (thelax.art/news/drers-revolutionary-engraving-techniques-explained.html), and tutorials formalize it as a parallel base tone with contour hatching layered on top for volume (lovelifedrawing.com/hatching).

## 3. Styles to steal (engraving-flavored, minimal, confident)

- Scratchboard is the closest analog, literally white-line-out-of-black engraving. Build by placing highlights not shadows, use directional parallel scratch-hatching for tone, leave large black areas bare for shadow, and cut crisp white lines to carve lit edges (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scratchboard).
- White-chalk heightening, the academic trois crayons approach on toned or black paper, assigns value by role, treating the board as shadow, stroke density as midtone, and pure bright chalk as highlight. Reserve the brightest white as sparing final accents so the whites stay precious (metmuseum.org/perspectives/materials-and-techniques-drawing-chalk).
- Kentridge charcoal animation covers the moving medium the other two lack. Steal erasure and boil, adding and partly rubbing marks back as the scroll advances so live ghost traces keep the chalk hand-alive rather than vector-clean (harvardfilmarchive.org/programs/the-animated-films-of-william-kentridge). Seurat's conte-on-Michallet is the pure-tone option for atmospheric zones (form from massed texture, no visible line), though it is the least linear fit for a hatching film (guggenheim-bilbao.eus/en/did-you-know/seurat-and-his-systematic-study-of-light).

## 4. Light-on-dark do's and don'ts

The classical logic inverts cleanly, so hatch where the light falls and leave the board as shadow (thevirtualinstructor.com/white-on-black.html).

Do build value from stroke density and spacing, not darker marks, since there is no dark medium to add. Do reserve the most opaque white for true highlights, and define an edge from its lit side with a thin bright contour line. Do keep strokes short, single-pass, and textured so the board tooth reads through.

Don't try to draw shadow or fill large darks, because adding grey muddies the black and kills the light-in-darkness depth. Leave big shadow areas bare (artistsnetwork.com/art-techniques/drawing-light-white-pencil-black-paper). Don't over-apply the white, since flooding greys the image and the whites lose their preciousness. Don't crosshatch or scribble for ordinary tone. Reserve a second angle for the deepest accents only, and let form-following, not crosshatching, carry volume.
